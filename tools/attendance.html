How it works

Your attendance is the share of classes you were present for. Enter how many classes have been held so far, how many you attended, and the minimum attendance your institution requires.

"Classes You Can Miss" counts future classes you could skip while staying at or above the requirement. "Classes You Need to Attend" is how many classes in a row you must attend, without missing any, to reach the requirement.

Want a step-by-step explanation? Read How to Calculate Attendance Percentage.

Formula

Attendance % = (Classes Attended ÷ Total Classes) × 100

Classes you can miss (future): ⌊ Attended × 100 ÷ Required − Total ⌋

Classes you need to attend: ⌈ (Required × Total − 100 × Attended) ÷ (100 − Required) ⌉

Example

Below the requirement: 50 classes held, 36 attended, 75% required.

Attendance = (36 ÷ 50) × 100 = 72%. You need 6 more classes in a row: (36 + 6) ÷ (50 + 6) = 42 ÷ 56 = 75%.

Above the requirement: 50 classes held, 45 attended, 75% required.

Attendance = (45 ÷ 50) × 100 = 90%. You can miss 10 more classes: 45 ÷ (50 + 10) = 45 ÷ 60 = 75%.

Tips

  • Keep a small buffer above the requirement in case of illness or emergencies.
  • Early absences hurt more: the total keeps growing, so a low start is hard to fix later.
  • Ask your department whether medical leave or approved absences are counted as present.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my data saved?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing you enter is sent to a server.

Why does it say "Not possible"?

If your requirement is 100% and you have already missed a class, you can no longer reach 100%, no matter how many classes you attend.

Does it round the result?

The percentage is shown to two decimal places. Class counts are always whole numbers, rounded in the safe direction.

Will my institution use the same rule?

Rules differ. Some institutions round up or allow condonation, so treat this as a guide and confirm with your institution.
