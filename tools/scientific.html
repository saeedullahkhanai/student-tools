How to Use the Scientific Calculator

Enter numbers and mathematical expressions using the calculator buttons or your keyboard. The calculator supports common arithmetic, trigonometric functions, logarithms, powers, roots, factorials, percentages and mathematical constants.

The result previews as you type. Press the equals button to complete a calculation and save it in the calculation history.

Scientific Calculator Functions

  • sin, cos and tan: Calculate trigonometric values.
  • sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹ and tan⁻¹: Calculate inverse trigonometric values.
  • ln and log: Calculate logarithms.
  • x²: Square a number.
  • xʸ: Calculate powers.
  • √: Calculate square roots.
  • n!: Calculate factorials.
  • π and e: Use common mathematical constants.
  • Ans: Reuse the previous answer.

Order of Operations

The calculator follows the normal order of mathematical operations.

Brackets → Functions and Powers → Multiplication and Division → Addition and Subtraction

Scientific Calculator Examples

2 × (3 + 4)² = 98

In Degree mode: sin(30) = 0.5

√(144) = 12

5! = 120

log(1000) = 3

Degrees and Radians

Trigonometric calculations can use either degrees or radians. Use Deg when your angle is expressed in degrees. Switch to Rad when working with radians.

For example, in Degree mode: sin(30) = 0.5.

Scientific Calculator Keyboard Shortcuts

  • Use number keys for digits.
  • Use +, -, *, / and ^ for common operations.
  • Use brackets for grouped expressions.
  • Press Enter to calculate.
  • Press Backspace to delete.
  • Press Escape to clear the calculator.

Tips for Using a Scientific Calculator

  • Check the Deg/Rad setting before using trigonometric functions.
  • Use brackets to make the order of your calculation clear.
  • Check the expression before pressing equals.
  • Use the history section to review previous calculations.
  • Use Ans when you want to continue from the previous result.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can a scientific calculator calculate?

This calculator supports arithmetic, trigonometric functions, logarithms, powers, square roots, factorials, percentages and constants such as pi and e.

What is the difference between Deg and Rad mode?

Degree mode interprets trigonometric angles in degrees, while radian mode interprets angles in radians. Choose the mode that matches your calculation.

How do I calculate a square root?

Use the square root button and enter the value inside the brackets. For example, √(144) is 12.

Can I use the calculator with my keyboard?

Yes. You can use digits, arithmetic operators, brackets, powers and other supported keyboard inputs. Enter calculates, Backspace deletes, and Escape clears.

Why might I see Invalid input?

An invalid result can occur when the expression is not supported or the mathematical operation is outside its valid range, such as a square root of a negative value or a factorial of an unsupported value.

Does the Scientific Calculator save my information?

Calculations are performed in your browser. No calculation data is sent to a server.
