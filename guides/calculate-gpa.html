How to Calculate GPA

Learn how to calculate semester GPA using credit hours, letter grades and grade points, with a simple worked example.

What Is GPA?

GPA stands for Grade Point Average. It represents your academic performance by combining the grade points you earn in your courses and weighting them according to credit hours.

A course with more credit hours has a greater effect on your GPA than a course with fewer credit hours.

This is a common 4.0 scale. Your university may use different grade-point values, so always check your institution's official grading policy.

GPA Formula

GPA = Σ (Credits × Grade Points) ÷ Σ Credits

The calculation first finds the quality points for each course. Quality points are calculated by multiplying the course credits by the grade points earned.

How to Calculate GPA Step by Step

  1. List all courses completed during the semester.
  2. Write the credit hours for each course.
  3. Convert each letter grade into its corresponding grade points.
  4. Multiply the credits by the grade points for every course.
  5. Add all quality points together.
  6. Add all course credits together.
  7. Divide total quality points by total credits.

Worked GPA Example

Suppose a student has three courses:

  • 3 credits with an A grade = 4.0 points
  • 4 credits with a B grade = 3.0 points
  • 3 credits with an A- grade = 3.7 points

Calculate the quality points:

(3 × 4.0) + (4 × 3.0) + (3 × 3.7) = 12 + 12 + 11.1 = 35.1

Total credits:

3 + 4 + 3 = 10 credits

Therefore:

GPA = 35.1 ÷ 10 = 3.51

The student's semester GPA is approximately 3.51.

Why Credit Hours Matter

GPA is normally credit-weighted. This means a 4-credit course affects your GPA more than a 1-credit course.

For this reason, simply adding grade points and dividing by the number of subjects may produce an incorrect result when courses have different credit hours.

Pass/Fail Courses

Pass/fail courses may be handled differently from standard letter-graded courses. Some institutions exclude them from GPA calculations, while others have specific rules.

Check your university's official academic policy before including these courses in your GPA calculation.

Common GPA Calculation Mistakes

  • Ignoring course credit hours.
  • Using the wrong grade-point value.
  • Simply averaging letter-grade points when credits are different.
  • Using a grading scale that does not match your university.
  • Including courses that your institution excludes from GPA.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is GPA?

GPA is the Grade Point Average, a credit-weighted measure of academic performance for a semester or another defined period.

How is GPA calculated?

Multiply each course's credits by its grade points, add the quality points, and divide by the total number of credits.

Why do credit hours matter in GPA?

Credit hours determine how strongly each course contributes to the final GPA.

Does every university use the same GPA scale?

No. Grading scales and academic policies vary between institutions. Check your university's official grading policy.
